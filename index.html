Structure of London Financial News

▼

News drives financial markets. The ICF is gathering key financial news databases and building AI analytical structures with the OCR data for research into key episodes in financial history.

Currently we have a near-complete corpus of text and embeddings for the Financial Times from 1888-2008, the Economist's Investors Monthly Manual from 1864-1930 and the Statist 1880-1930. These comprise more than 5 million pages of text and form the core of an engine to study the evolution of financial news journalism over 150 years.

wng-yale.github.io/structure-london-financial-news

Money Markets

▼

This project brings together all available information about the world's stock, bond and commodity exchanges, as well as their historical and ancient antecedents. It is an encyclopedic resource for the study and exploration of the origins, development, impact, evolution and transformation of financial markets.

It is the work of a team of Yale students over the year 2025-6 and the basis for a book project to bring art historians, architects, economists and others together to produce an illustrated volume like the Origins of Value or The Great Mirror of Folly.

wng-yale.github.io/money-markets

Structure of Russian Financial News

▼

A companion to The Structure of London Financial News, applying the same computational-history toolkit to the imperial Russian financial press. Built around a digitized run of Vestnik Finansov, Promyshlennosti i Torgovli — the official weekly of the Russian Ministry of Finance, published in St. Petersburg from 1883 to 1917.

Roughly 77,510 OCR'd pages and 62 million words spanning 1869 to 1916. Features bilingual English–Russian search, NMF topic model, hierarchical taxonomy dendrogram, monthly topic prevalence, and Procrustes-aligned diachronic FastText embeddings for Cyrillic OCR.

wng-yale.github.io/structure-russian-financial-news

Structure of Shanghai Financial News

▼

A companion to The Structure of London and Russian Financial News, extending the same computational-history toolkit to the English-language financial press of treaty-port China. Built around the North China Herald, the leading English-language weekly of Shanghai, digitized from the Internet Archive's public-domain collection and spanning 1850–1941.

A 50-topic Non-negative Matrix Factorization model with a commercial-lexicon finance flag surfaces financial themes alongside general news. Features a topic explorer with yearly attention patterns, a financial-topics filter, a taxonomy dendrogram and correlation heatmap, and decade-based financial-attention trajectories — laying groundwork for comparison with the forthcoming Chinese-language Shenbao dataset.

wng-yale.github.io/structure-shanghai-financial-news

Dow Theory — Hamilton Agent

▼

The Hamilton agent operates daily. Each US trading day after the close, the agent ingests current market indicators and retrieves the four 1903–1929 Hamilton editorials whose market state is most similar to today's by FEVA distance. Claude Opus writes a Hamilton-voiced editorial diagnosing the primary trend, secondary movement, and rails-industrials confirmation.

Six reader-archetype rules translate the structured diagnosis into daily long-DJI position weights. A scoreboard reports each archetype's running performance under Sharpe, Sortino, max drawdown, MPPM, and the Henriksson-Merton timing coefficient. The site is regenerated from an audit-trail JSONL and republished automatically by GitHub Actions.

wng-yale.github.io/hamilton-agent

IMM N-gram Viewer

▼

An interactive n-gram viewer for exploring word frequency trends in the Investor's Monthly Manual, 1864–1930. A companion tool to the Structure of London Financial News project, enabling researchers to trace the rise and fall of financial terminology, company names, and economic concepts across nearly seven decades of the premier Victorian and Edwardian investment periodical.

wng-yale.github.io/imm-ngrams

Shiller Investor Survey Dashboard

▼

Interactive dashboard for the Shiller Individual Investor Survey, a long-running Yale School of Management project tracking investor beliefs, expectations, and confidence. Based on questionnaire survey data collected since 1989 under the direction of Professor Robert Shiller and the Yale International Center for Finance.

wng-yale.github.io/shiller-survey-dashboard

Shiller U.S. Confidence Indices

▼

The U.S. Stock Market Confidence Indices, based on monthly survey data collected since 1989 by the Yale International Center for Finance. Reports raw monthly results from samples of wealthy individual investors and institutional investors, covering crash confidence, valuation confidence, buy-on-dips confidence, and one-year confidence.

yale-icf.github.io/ShillerUSConfidence
